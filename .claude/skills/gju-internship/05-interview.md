# Interview preparation

STAR structure and the personalised-examples pattern are from ai-job-search (MIT).
The question bank is GJU's own — Part 4 sets out the five phases a German
interview actually follows, in German, and it is more specific than any generic
guide. Source:
`src/assets/gy-internships/application-process/praktikumsinfo4_bewerbungsgespraech_en_neues_logo.pdf`,
written out on the home page under "The interview".

## STAR, and where it does and does not apply

**Situation · Task · Action · Result.** One to two minutes. End with what you
learned or would do differently.

Build every example from `data/experience.json`, `data/certificates.json` and
`data/profile.json`. If the student has few roles, use coursework, the 160-hour
Field Training, a project or voluntary work — Part 1 says the same about the CV.
**Never invent a result.** An unquantified real outcome beats an invented metric,
and the audit rule in `03-anschreiben.md` applies here too: if it is not in the
data files, ask the student and write it back before using it.

Prepare **three** examples covering teamwork, a technical problem, and a
difficulty or failure. German interviewers ask for all three.

## The five phases (Part 4)

Rehearse in German if the advert is German.

**1 · Begrüßung und Einleitung** — `Haben Sie gut hierher gefunden?`
Small talk. Have one sentence ready; do not fill silence.

**2 · Kennenlernen des Bewerbers** — the long one.
- `Erzählen Sie uns etwas über sich und Ihren bisherigen Werdegang.`
- `Warum haben Sie sich für dieses Studium entschieden? Warum an der GJU?`
- `Warum sind Sie zum Studieren nach Deutschland gekommen?`
- `Was ist Ihr berufliches Ziel?`
- `Was sind Ihre Stärken? Und Ihre Schwächen?`
- `Was machen Sie in Ihrer Freizeit?`

**3 · Unternehmen und Praktikumsinhalte**
- `Was wissen Sie über unser Unternehmen?` — read the site and recent news
  beforehand: employee count, divisions, products, current projects.
- `Warum haben Sie sich gerade bei uns beworben?`
- `Was erwarten Sie von Ihrem Praktikum?`
- `Welche Fragen haben Sie an uns?`

**4 · Vertragsverhandlungen** — start date, duration, `Praktikantenvergütung`.
This is where the 20 weeks get confirmed. Do not leave it vague.

**5 · Abschluss** — `Wie geht es weiter? Bis wann ist eine Entscheidung zu erwarten?`

## Answers worth rehearsing

**Strengths and weaknesses.** Pair a weakness with a matching strength. Avoid the
worn-out "I expect too much of myself" — Part 4 names it as a stock phrase.
Saying your German is not perfect but improving is honest and lands well.

**Where in 2, 5, 10 years.** Be concrete: a master's in Germany, a direction.
Tie it back to them where you honestly can.

## Questions to ask them

Keep one or two for the end. Open questions — *who, what, how*. Avoid *why*: it
can sound like criticism.

How are interns supervised here · Which departments will I see · Which tasks ·
Who are my colleagues · What does the workplace look like · You mentioned project
X on your site, can you tell me more · Could you help me find a room in …

Pay and working-time rules belong before signing, not necessarily in the first
conversation.

## What need not be answered

Finances, marriage, wish for children, pregnancy, illness, religion. Anything
unrelated to the job. Exceptions: an illness relevant to the work, a contagious
disease, or a condition likely to make you unfit for work must be disclosed on
request.

## Practicalities

Arrive ~15 minutes early. Bring a copy of the application, the invitation, and
copies of certificates and references. Refuse an offered cigarette politely; a
drink is fine. Feet flat, well back on the chair, no crossed legs, no folded arms.
Talk clearly more than half the time. Ask at the start whether you may take notes.

## Output

Produce a one-page brief per interview: the company facts to know, three STAR
examples matched to the likely questions, the German phrasing for phases 2 and 3,
two questions to ask, and the contract points to settle in phase 4.
